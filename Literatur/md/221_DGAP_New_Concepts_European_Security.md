# New Concepts for a New Era of European Security

**Report by German Council on Foreign Relations (DGAP)**

**Date:** February 20, 2026

**Authors:** Dr. Stefan Meister, Dr. Mario Baumann, Ricarda Nierhaus

---

## Overview

This DGAP report presents consolidated recommendations from the European Zeitenwende Strategy Group addressing how Europe can strengthen its capacity for independent security provision. The analysis encompasses four interconnected resilience dimensions: security and defense, economic stability, societal cohesion, and institutional governance.

## Key Recommendations

### Security and Defense

The report emphasizes that "European agency on security cannot rely on military means alone but requires a comprehensive approach." Priority actions include:

- Establishing coordinated deterrence messaging toward Russia among willing European partners
- Expanding integrated air and missile defense capabilities on NATO's eastern boundary
- Reducing European dependence on U.S. strategic enablers through enhanced defense investments
- Increasing defense research funding, particularly for drone technology and anti-drone systems
- Modernizing NATO nuclear strategy and reintroducing crisis simulation exercises

### Economic Resilience

Recommendations focus on industrial coordination and sustainable Ukraine support:

- Joint procurement initiatives leveraging Nordic and Baltic country expertise
- Strengthening critical infrastructure protection across ports, energy grids, and maritime assets
- Establishing substantial EU funding for Ukraine's defense within the 2028-2034 financial framework
- Co-locating European defense production in Ukraine to accelerate innovation cycles
- Clarifying export procedures to integrate Ukraine into European supply chains

### Societal Resilience

The strategy emphasizes rebuilding public consensus through:

- Developing positive narratives reconnecting societies and political leadership
- Transparent communication about defense-spending tradeoffs
- Highlighting domestic benefits including job creation and technological advancement
- Strengthening public understanding of Russian strategic objectives to build resistance against hybrid interference

### Institutional Reform

Structural improvements target enhanced EU responsiveness:

- Adopting multi-annual assistance frameworks bypassing unanimous approval requirements
- Implementing qualified majority voting for Common Security and Defence Policy decisions
- Establishing differentiated burden-sharing mechanisms across European states
- Increasing intelligence-sharing to expose and counter hybrid attacks

## Strategic Context

The group operated under the premise that "Ukraine's survival as a sovereign state" represents the foundational security requirement conditioning all other European initiatives. Membership included experts from Central Europe, Eastern Europe, Nordic states, and Baltic nations — regions with the most direct security stakes.
