# NATO Militaries Strengthening Allied Medical Resilience

**07 April 2026 | 2 MIN READ**

## Overview

NATO member states elevated medical readiness standards this winter through coordinated exercises designed to strengthen cooperation between military and civilian healthcare systems during crises or armed conflict.

## Key Leadership Statement

Brigadier General Petter Iversen, Chair of the Committee of Chiefs of Military Medical Services in NATO (COMEDS), emphasized that "medical support is not an add-on to operations, but a core element of Allied deterrence" and stressed the necessity of "full-scale war takes an immense toll on health."

## Major Exercises Conducted

Three significant training operations demonstrated medical interoperability:

**Cold Response (Norway)**
- Arctic-based exercise testing large-scale wartime health readiness
- Personnel transported wounded across long distances and borders
- Combination of live training and virtual components

**Medic Quadriga 26 (Germany)**
- Focused on complete medical evacuation chain
- Coordinated from front-line operations to strategic aeromedical transfer
- Integrated civilian healthcare providers

**Casualty Move 26 (CAMO)**
- Led by Multinational Medical Coordination Centre – Europe
- Training for mass patient management and command control
- Multi-location exercise with reach-back coordination cells

## Strategic Message

Healthcare resilience requires whole-of-society engagement, with the emphasis that "Health Security is Collective Security," reflecting lessons from recent conflict impacts on military forces and civilian populations.
