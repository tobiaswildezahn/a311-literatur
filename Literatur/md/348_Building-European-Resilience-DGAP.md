# Building European Resilience and Capacity to Act: Lessons for 2030

## Publication Details

**Title:** Building European Resilience and Capacity to Act: Lessons for 2030
**Authors:** Roderick Parkes, Anna-Lena Kirch, Serafine Dinkel
**Publication Date:** December 15, 2021
**Type:** DGAP Report No. 13
**Organization:** German Council on Foreign Relations (DGAP)

## Abstract

This foresight report examines how the European Union can maintain its capacity to act amid major disruptive changes anticipated through 2030. Through scenario analysis across four critical domains, the authors identify pathways for European resilience.

## Key Arguments

### Core Thesis
The EU must shift from linear, reactive policymaking to nimble, adaptive governance that leverages disruptions as opportunities for positive change rather than threats to manage.

### Three Central Lessons

**1. Embrace Disruption as Opportunity**
The report argues that "the EU must look upon disruptions as a means to change policy course." Status quo approaches consistently underperform compared to scenarios where policymakers fundamentally reassess assumptions.

**2. Break Policy Silos**
Successful resilience requires integrated approaches across technology, security, economics, and migration rather than compartmentalized responses.

**3. Prioritize Relationships Over Autonomy**
The pursuit of "European autonomy" through market closure and unilateral regulation appears counterproductive. Best-case scenarios result from open-ended investments in international partnerships and domestic cohesion.

## Four Policy Fields Analyzed

### Digital Technologies
Innovation should precede regulation; regulatory overreach stifles European competitiveness while early investment in accessible technologies builds market leadership.

### Trade & Geo-Economics
Rather than competing with the United States and China, the EU should pursue issue-specific alliances that distribute influence beyond great power competition.

### Emerging Security Threats
Security cooperation enables climate adaptation and technology transfer; unilateral climate commitments alone prove insufficient.

### Large-Scale Migration
European competitiveness for global talent depends on building robust African labor markets rather than defensive border policies that paradoxically increase irregular migration.

## Structural Findings

**Five Key Takeaways:**
- All policy fields interconnect; isolated approaches miss leverage points
- Multilateral cooperation quality determines outcomes across all scenarios
- Domestic EU cohesion proves decisive for crisis response effectiveness
- Status quo and worst-case scenarios display surprising similarity
- Strategic action, not structural determinism, shapes 2030 outcomes

## Critical Context

The report responds to concerning European trajectories: European companies represent less than 4% of major digital firms' market capitalization; the EU imports 78% of lithium from Chile; demographic decline reduces Europe's global population share from 20% to 8.5% by 2030.

The authors contend that such trajectories remain changeable through deliberate policy innovation rather than fatalistic adaptation to predetermined trends.
