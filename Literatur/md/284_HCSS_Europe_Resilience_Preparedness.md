# Assessing Europe's Resilience and Preparedness in an Era of Strategic Risks

**December 2, 2025**

## Overview

Europe faces security challenges combining military threats with climate, economic, and technological disruptions. A new HCSS report co-authored with the Centre for Security, Diplomacy and Strategy examines ten EU and NATO member states through the lens of the EU's Preparedness Union Strategy.

## Key Findings

The research reveals that European nations strengthen resilience domains recently tested by crises while leaving other areas vulnerable. The study identifies civil-military cooperation as essential—when institutionalized, it multiplies effectiveness; when ad hoc, it becomes a crisis bottleneck.

Critical vulnerabilities include fuel supply constraints, transport infrastructure gaps, rail-gauge incompatibilities, and 5G and undersea cable exposure. These weaknesses accumulate across the EU-NATO bloc, potentially hindering deterrence and force deployment.

## Policy Recommendations

**EU-NATO Level:**
- Establish an EU-NATO Fuel Assurance Compact with redundant storage
- Expand dual-use infrastructure funding and coordinate rail transitions
- Create undersea cable intelligence task force

**Netherlands-Specific:**
- Lead green-defense resilience initiatives
- Build Port of Rotterdam redundancy
- Expand critical-infrastructure protection to partner nations

## Conclusion

Lead author Hans Horan states: "Europe has the political will and institutional tools to build a genuine Preparedness Union. But unless we close the gap between ambition and delivery, the weakest links in our resilience systems will continue to jeopardise the collective security."

Resilience represents a core strategic capability fundamental to deterrence, crisis response, and democratic stability.
