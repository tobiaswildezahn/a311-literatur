# Action Plan for Security and Defense Policy

**Autoren:** Dr. Claudia Major, Dr. Constanze Stelzenmüller, Dr. Christian Mölling
**Datum:** 21. September 2021
**Institution:** Deutsche Gesellschaft für Auswärtige Politik (DGAP)
**Quelle:** https://dgap.org/en/research/publications/action-plan-security-and-defense-policy

---

## Overview

This report argues that Germany's security is fundamentally threatened by deteriorating global conditions. As an interconnected nation, Germany must comprehensively overhaul its security policy or risk losing strategic autonomy to rivals like China and Russia.

## Key Context

Post-Cold War assumptions about global convergence toward Western models have proven false. Germany mistakenly believed economic interdependence ensured peace, overlooking that partners can become adversaries—exemplified by Russia's 2014 Crimea annexation. The strategic environment has fundamentally shifted.

### Emerging Threats

- China's pursuit of global dominance and Russian territorial expansion
- U.S. ambivalence about its security guarantor role
- Novel weapons technologies (hypersonic missiles, AI, autonomous systems)
- Information operations using digital platforms

> "The obvious centrifugal forces in the Western Alliance and EU make Germany the fulcrum at which the European order could be overturned"

## Strategic Culture and Institutional Reforms

### 1. Invigorate Germany's Strategic Culture

Germany's limited conception of feasible security options constrains policymaking. Political leaders face cultural resistance when proposing actions outside accepted frameworks. The report argues that expanding "what is conceivable" requires whole-of-government action with public participation.

### 2. Establish a Federal Security Council

The existing body, focused narrowly on weapons exports, should transform into a comprehensive coordination mechanism. It should include:

- Cabinet committee for discussion and decisions
- Secretariat combining government officials with academic experts
- Regular engagement on strategic issues across ministries

### 3. Create Independent Security Commission

An expert body composed of parliamentarians, scholars, and officials should produce annual assessments of security risks and policy recommendations within one year.

### 4. Democratize Security Policy

The public must participate in developing security visions. Suggested measures include:

- National security policy year or semester
- Annual security week in parliament
- New Bundestag committee on security policy
- Annual chancellor speech on security strategy

## Policy Reforms

### 1. Integrate NATO and EU Strengths

The two organizations rarely coordinate despite 21 shared members. The report urges:

- Common security analysis by summer 2022
- European Joint Force providing 50% of conventional European defense capabilities
- Coordinated procurement and technological development
- Department for "Euro-Atlantic security" at the Foreign Office

### 2. Ensure Funding Predictability

Germany repeatedly pledges NATO's 2% target but falls short. Solutions include:

- Meeting the 2% commitment by 2024 to restore credibility
- Five-to-ten-year "Bundeswehr Planning Act"
- Four-year equipment initiative for operational readiness
- Reserve 0.5% GDP for joint EU-NATO projects

### 3. Shape Nuclear Order and Deterrence

Germany lacks nuclear weapons but benefits from NATO's nuclear umbrella. Recommendations:

- Promote intermediate-range missile disarmament in Europe
- Maintain credible nuclear sharing with U.S. forces
- Engage France and UK in strategic nuclear dialogue
- Purchase next-generation combat aircraft from the United States

### 4. Strengthen Crisis Prevention and Stabilization

Afghanistan's failure demands reassessment. Key actions:

- Comprehensive review of operations in Afghanistan, Mali, and others
- Strategic planning through 2040 on likely conflict scenarios
- European stabilization corps with 5,000 German specialists
- Participatory learning process involving civil society

### 5. Reorganize Military Technology Exports

Current export controls lack transparency and reliability. Proposed framework:

- Security-policy justification for all exports
- Country-specific strategies using traffic-light system (green/yellow/red)
- Statutory export law covering weapons and emerging technologies
- International initiative for controlling critical technology exports

### 6. Build Resilience Across Society

Civil society and military must integrate defensive capabilities. Measures:

- Regular exercises and simulations at all government levels
- Cross-sectoral drills testing local-state-federal coordination
- Stress testing of critical state institutions

## Conclusion

Germany faces unprecedented security challenges requiring institutional reform, cultural shift, and resource commitment. Without decisive action, strategic challengers will exploit European divisions. The new government must begin immediately with mind-set changes, implement structural reforms, and adapt specific policies to meet 21st-century threats.
