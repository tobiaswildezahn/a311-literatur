# NATO and Societal Resilience: All Hands on Deck in an Age of War

**Date:** July 20, 2022
**Authors:** Edward Hunter Christie, Kristine Berzina
**Source:** German Marshall Fund of the United States (gmfus.org)
**URL:** https://www.gmfus.org/news/nato-and-societal-resilience-all-hands-deck-age-war

---

## Summary

This policy brief examines how NATO and member states can strengthen societal resilience – the capacity of nations to withstand crises, adapt to shocks, and recover from armed attacks while protecting civilians and supporting defense operations.

---

## Key Findings

### Current NATO Framework

NATO established seven baseline requirements for national resilience in 2016:

1. Continuity of government and critical services
2. Resilient energy supplies
3. Management of uncontrolled population movement
4. Resilient food and water resources
5. Mass casualty and health crisis response
6. Resilient civil communications systems
7. Resilient transport systems

### Proposed Additions

The authors recommend adding three new baseline requirements:

- **Payment systems resilience** – essential for military operations and civilian transactions
- **Psychological defense** – countering disinformation and maintaining civilian morale and will to defend
- **Data and infrastructure software continuity** – protecting digital assets, with lessons from Ukraine's experience dispersing government data to secure cloud storage outside national borders

---

## EU Coordination

The EU's proposed Critical Entities Resilience Directive covers ten sectors (energy, transport, banking, health, water, digital infrastructure, communications, public administration, and space). The brief emphasizes the need for liaison between NATO and EU institutions to ensure alignment on threat assessments, particularly regarding armed attack scenarios.

---

## Finland and Sweden's Contributions

Both countries demonstrate comprehensive whole-of-society defense approaches:

- **Sweden's total defense** involves all citizens aged 16–70 in potential crisis response, with emphasis on individual responsibility and self-sufficiency
- **Finland's comprehensive security** model prioritizes cross-sector collaboration and citizen engagement through initiatives like the "72-hour concept"

NATO should learn from their approaches to: breaking down barriers between civilian-military actors, fostering interdependence across government, and communicating directly with populations about resilience expectations.

---

## Key Recommendation

> "The ability of societies to withstand and adapt in crises and emergencies is an essential element of national security" – requiring coordinated efforts across government, private sector, civil society, and citizens.

The brief argues resilience depends not only on technical infrastructure but also on intangible factors like social trust and the willingness of populations to support collective defense.
