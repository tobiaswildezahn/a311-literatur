# Resilience Spending Must Strengthen Europe's Security – Not Become an Accounting Exercise

**Published:** 4 March 2026
**Source:** Heinrich-Böll-Stiftung European Union / RUSI
**Authors:** Dan Marks, Ed Arnold (RUSI)

---

## Overview

NATO allies committed to spending 1.5% of GDP on resilience at the 2025 NATO Summit, alongside a 3.5% core defence target. However, a new paper warns that "without proper rules and definitions, that money risks being swallowed by bureaucracy."

## Key Arguments

The paper *Sustainability for Resilience* emphasizes that resilience investments must serve dual purposes.

Strategic investments in rail mobility, energy grid upgrades, decentralized renewables, supply-chain diversification, and nature restoration can simultaneously enhance military capabilities, reduce fossil fuel dependency, boost industrial competitiveness, and support climate objectives.

## Framework Recommendation

The authors propose implementing a role-based framework that aligns investments with each NATO member's defensive function — whether frontline, transit, littoral, or less exposed. They also call for National Resilience Plans directly connected to NATO regional defence plans.

## Expert Commentary

Roderick Kefferpütz, Director of Heinrich-Böll-Stiftung EU, stated: "investments in energy systems, rail infrastructure or even nature-based barriers can significantly enhance NATO's defence."
