# Germany's Path to Kriegstüchtigkeit: The 2026 Defence Budget

**Date:** December 19, 2025
**Author:** Levente Bartha

## Overview

Germany approved a €524.54 billion budget for 2026, allocating €82.69 billion to the Bundeswehr—roughly 15% of total spending. Combined with a Special Fund, total defence expenditure reaches approximately €108 billion. This represents a €20.2 billion increase from 2025 and signals Berlin's commitment to becoming "the strongest conventional army in Europe."

## Budget Breakdown and Key Takeaways

The Bundeswehr's 2026 budget defines five primary operational tasks:

1. National and collective defense within NATO
2. International crisis management contributions
3. Homeland security and domestic preparedness
4. Partnerships and cooperation
5. Humanitarian relief and disaster assistance

Military procurement experienced the largest budget increase at €16.8 billion, representing 27.06% of defence spending. Key allocations include:

- €1.5 billion for vehicles and accessories
- €1.89 billion for field equipment
- €1.4 billion for the PUMA Infantry Fighting Vehicle fleet
- €15 billion for ammunition
- Billions for space and cyber capabilities

## Lessons from Ukraine

The conflict has prompted Germany to prioritize intelligence and surveillance infrastructure, recognizing that "the modern battlefield is transparent." Berlin is investing substantially in space and cyber assets to address capability gaps.

The dramatically increased ammunition consumption in Ukraine compared to previous conflicts necessitates the €15 billion allocation, though European and NATO manufacturers face production bottlenecks.

## Procurement Reform

Defence Minister Boris Pistorius stated: "There is no reason to complain anymore," emphasizing Berlin's commitment to streamlined procurement. The proposed "Bundeswehr Planning and Procurement Acceleration Act" (BwPBBG) is expected to take effect in early 2026, simplifying delivery processes and signaling long-term rearmament commitment to defence contractors.

## Geopolitical Implications

**For Russia:** The budget demonstrates Berlin's renewed NATO dedication, though meaningful capability expansion will require years to materialize. Russian ammunition production currently exceeds NATO output.

**For the United States:** The spending reaffirms Germany's willingness to shoulder greater collective defence responsibilities, potentially enabling Berlin greater policy influence within NATO structures.

**For NATO/Europe:** A strengthened Bundeswehr will enhance Germany's leadership capacity, including its commitment to station a permanent heavy brigade in Lithuania and lead regional military exercises.

## Conclusion

While representing significant progress, the 2026 budget constitutes a single step in Germany's longer rearmament trajectory. Realizing procurement reforms, expanding industrial capacity, and integrating new equipment into operational structures will require sustained commitment across multiple budget cycles.
