# Geopolitical Hybrid Threats

**Authors:** Norbert Metiu, Martin Völpel

**Date:** 30.03.2026

**Publication:** Bundesbank Discussion Paper 11/2026

**DOI:** doi.org/10.71734/DP-2026–11

## Abstract

The authors introduce a novel index measuring geopolitical hybrid threats (GHT) and examine their macroeconomic effects. They find that hybrid threats primarily weaken aggregate demand by raising uncertainty, eroding confidence, and tightening financial conditions. Policymakers respond with increased defense spending and monetary easing.

## Key Findings

**Definition and Scope**

Hybrid threats encompass cyberattacks, infrastructure sabotage, espionage, economic coercion, and disinformation campaigns operating below overt warfare thresholds. Recent examples include the 2014 Crimea annexation, 2016 U.S. election interference, 2017 WannaCry attacks, and 2022 Nord Stream pipeline sabotage.

**The GHT Index**

The researchers developed a quantitative measure using textual analysis of ten major English-language newspapers from 1985-2026, tracking terms related to critical infrastructure sabotage, economic coercion, espionage, and disinformation. The index reveals a dramatic surge since the mid-2010s, exceeding late Cold War levels.

**Economic Mechanisms**

Hybrid threat shocks operate as negative demand shocks: economic activity declines, prices fall, business and consumer sentiment deteriorates, and uncertainty rises. Financial conditions tighten, raising corporate financing costs. Governments respond with monetary easing and expanded defense budgets funded through increased public debt.

**Transmission Channels**

Unlike traditional geopolitical risks with supply-side effects, hybrid threats operate primarily through demand-side channels — functioning similarly to uncertainty shocks that prompt economic caution among businesses and households.
