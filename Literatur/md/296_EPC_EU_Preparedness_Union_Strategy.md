# European Preparedness Union Strategy – A Step Closer to EU's Comprehensive Security Needs?

## Overview

The European Commission and High Representative have published a Joint Communication on the European Preparedness Union Strategy, establishing parameters for a crisis preparedness framework. This represents a shift from fragmented security approaches toward a more integrated vision encompassing energy, food, climate, and health security alongside traditional defense concerns.

## Core Argument

While the strategy signals important progress, significant governance weaknesses threaten its implementation. The EU27 lacks unanimous commitment, and coordination deficits across policy areas remain unresolved.

## Key Vulnerabilities Identified

### Governance Gaps

The Communication mentions "security" 44 times but falls short of clarifying how fragmented policy structures should be addressed. Recent policy actions — including Cohesion Policy mid-term reviews — continue operating in isolation rather than supporting comprehensive preparedness goals.

### Regional Dimension Overlooked

Ironically, while the strategy calls for improved governance, proposed budget architecture ("Single National Plans") risks centralizing authority with member states and marginalizing regional actors. This contradicts the strategy's recognition that local knowledge proves essential for:

- Identifying vulnerable populations
- Coordinating civilian infrastructure support
- Developing comprehensive crisis management frameworks
- Ensuring crisis communications reach all territories

## Recommendations

**Priority Actions:**

1. Upgrade EU policy governance and coordination by integrating all security-related investments into one comprehensive architecture
2. Strengthen multi-level governance by embedding regions as critical players in preparedness frameworks
3. Position comprehensive security at the core of post-2027 budget redesign
4. Redesign Single National Plans to preserve regional voice and capacity

## Conclusion

Success requires unanimous EU27 commitment and political leadership to overcome coordination deficits and territorial fragmentation.

## Einordnung für A311

Die EPC-Analyse ist für die Hamburger Krisenvorsorge politisch hochrelevant: Wenn die EU-Architektur tatsächlich ueber "Single National Plans" zentralisiert wird, hätte Hamburg als Stadtstaat einen direkten Zugriff auf die Bundes-Schnittstelle — Flächenländer wären strukturell benachteiligt. Umgekehrt droht der Stadtstaat aus Sicht der EU-Strategie als regionaler Akteur "wegzufallen", wenn der Bund das Mandat allein wahrnimmt. Das Argument der EPC, regionale Verantwortliche stärker einzubinden, deckt sich mit der Hamburger Position.
